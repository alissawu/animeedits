# Edit analysis

How `scene_frames/analyze_edit.py` turns a short reference edit (15–40 s) into the measured timeline a breakdown is written from: the timings and the cut-by-cut table in `breakdowns/`. Given a song file instead, it maps the audio alone, so an agent can plan a new edit on it.

The song map says where a song turns as facts about what changes: which stems play when, where drums or bass come in or stop, where the band drops away under the voice, where everything stops, where a part stops repeating its loop, the bass root per bar, and how high and loud the voice sits per section. It never labels a moment with a mood or a section name like "chorus"; what a turn means for the edit is the editor's call. The methods and thresholds come from the song-analysis trial in `out/song-analysis-trial/report.md` (main checkout only), which checked them against lane plots, the White Light breakdown and five reference edits.

## Usage

```sh
python scene_frames/analyze_edit.py MEDIA OUT_DIR [--caption-band 0.75:1] [--no-words] [--no-texture] [--stem-cache DIR]
```

MEDIA is a video, or an audio file such as mp3, wav, m4a or flac. Cover art in an audio file is not treated as video. For audio-only input, the visual stages are skipped and OUT_DIR gets `report.md` and `timeline.json` with the audio map, word timings and song sections.

Requires ffmpeg on PATH and `requirements.txt`. Downloads on first use: the htdemucs stem model (80 MB, to `~/.cache/animeedits/separator/`), faster-whisper `small.en` (about 460 MB) and three Essentia tagger models (23 MB, to `~/.cache/animeedits/essentia/`, checked against pinned SHA-256 sums). Audio and video stay local.

A four-minute song takes about 3 minutes the first time on an M2 Max: 25–40 s to separate stems, about 10 s for the measurements and the rest for Whisper. Reruns read the caches. `--no-words` skips Whisper and `--no-texture` skips the key and tags.

Writes to OUT_DIR:

- `report.md`: the skeleton (key and tempo, the drop, when each stem plays, section boundaries, the voice between sections, vocal phrases), the turns table and stops, then sections: for a song, a table with the stems playing and the voice in each; for a video, pacing per section and one row per shot, flash or stretch of black. Each picture row gives in/out, length, how the shot was entered, and the offset to the nearest beat, strong onset and word edge. The audio detail at the end has the loudness table, bass roots per bar, beats, strong onsets and word timings.
- `timeline.json`: the same data for later stages. `media` holds the probe. `picture` (null for audio) holds segments with frame numbers, in-shot events and dissolves. `audio` holds tempo and `tempo_alternative`, beats, strong onsets, loudness windows, `stems` (runs per stem), `turns` (each with its stem `events` and its `mix_db` and `low_db` change), `drops` (the drop first), `stops`, section `boundaries`, `harmony` (bass root per bar), `voice` (per section) and `voice_shifts`. Then come `words`, `phrases`, `sections`, `sheets`, `texture` (key, instrument and genre tags) and `settings`. frames.py reads `audio.beats`, `audio.drops[].time` and `words`.
- `frames.json`: per-frame luma, white share, contrast, histogram distance, pixel difference, structure and edge distance, and the frame's label.
- `sheets/shots_NN.jpg`: one keyframe per shot, labelled `S4 6.95-7.85s`.
- `sheets/captions_NN.jpg`: the caption band every 0.25 s at up to 1280 px wide, so burned-in captions can be read. Use `--caption-band TOP:BOTTOM` (fractions of the frame height) to move or tighten it, and `--caption-step` to change the interval.

No lyric or dialogue text is written anywhere. Whisper's words are dropped as soon as their timings are read.

## How it works

1. **Frames.** Every frame is decoded through ffmpeg's `fps` filter at 128 px on the long side, so frame *i* is at *i* / fps. Per frame the script records mean luma, the share of pixels above 200, the 98th-percentile luma, an RGB histogram distance and the mean pixel difference to the previous frame. It also records two brightness-normalised signatures: standardised half-size luma ("structure") and standardised gradient magnitude ("edges").
2. **Labels.** A frame is *white* when its mean luma is at least 140 and at least 25% of its pixels are above 200. It is *black* when 98% of its pixels are below 30 and the mean is below 10. A white run longer than 0.5 s is bright footage, not a flash. A run of white or black frames becomes a transition span. Any footage inside it shorter than the minimum shot (two frames) is treated as a strobe and stays in the span.
3. **Cuts.** Between adjacent footage frames, a cut needs both the structure distance and the edge distance to reach 0.45 (`--cut-threshold`). The distances are the smallest over the two frames on each side, so one flicker frame cannot fake a cut, and a mean pixel change under 3 never cuts. Across a flash or black span the default flips: it is a cut unless both distances fall below 0.35.
   Two shots alike in layout and palette can fall short of that bar, so a second test looks inside each stretch the first one left uncut. A frame there is a cut when its pixel change is at least 2.5 times every other change within 0.3 s in the same stretch, its edge distance reaches 0.55, and its histogram distance reaches 0.15. Animation on 2s and 3s, fast action and shake change on many nearby frames, so no single frame stands out. A picture the editor nudges or zooms in a step stands out but keeps its colours, and a grade stepped over a held picture keeps most of its edges. A stretch needs 0.3 s of other frames to measure against, so a run of one- and two-frame shots is left to the first test.
4. **Short pieces.** A piece shorter than two frames becomes a flash if it is at least 40 luma brighter than both neighbours. Otherwise it joins the incoming shot. When a flash or black span separates it from the incoming shot, it joins whichever side it resembles.
5. **In-shot events.** A flash or black span with the same shot on both sides is listed as an in-shot event, not a cut.
6. **Dissolves.** Inside each shot, windows of ±0.1–0.4 s whose ends differ like a cut are fitted as a linear blend of their two end frames. A window passes when the blend residual is at most 0.35 of the distance to the nearer end and the mix only moves one way. The dissolve is the union of the blended frames of the passing windows that overlap the best one. It belongs to the incoming shot, which starts on its first blended frame.
7. **Audio.** librosa gives the tempo and beat grid, and the onsets at or above the 90th percentile of onset strength. RMS and a 150 Hz low-pass are reported in dBFS per 0.25 s window. A tempo above 140 or below 75 BPM is flagged with its half or double, since beat trackers often count twice or half the beat a listener taps (the trial saw 184.6 for a 92 BPM song and 58.7 for a 122 one). The onset signal can't settle which is right, so check by tapping along.
8. **Stems.** `stems.py` separates the first audio track into drums, bass, other (guitars, keys, synths) and vocals with Demucs v4 `htdemucs`, through audio-separator. The input is scaled under 0.5 peak so audio-separator's output normalisation never changes a stem's level, then scaled back. Stems are cached as mono FLAC at 22.05 kHz in `~/.cache/animeedits/stems/htdemucs/<key>/`, keyed by the file's content, so every output folder and worktree shares them (`--stem-cache` moves it).
9. **When each stem plays.** Each stem's RMS per 0.25 s, max-filtered over 0.75 s so a sparse drum part counts as playing between hits, is compared with a threshold 20 dB under the stem's own 95th percentile (never below −50 dBFS). Runs shorter than 2 s (3 s for vocals) are absorbed into their neighbours. A stem whose 95th percentile is under −42 dBFS isn't in the song. One 20 dB or more under the mix's 95th percentile is faint, probably bleed from another stem: it gets runs but no events. Each run edge is then refined to the 50 ms block where the level crosses the threshold and snapped to a beat within 0.12 s. Without that refinement, the envelope made entries 0.25–0.5 s early, and the trial caught it against an editor's flash on the bass entry.
10. **Turns.** Stem events grouped within 1.25 s of a group's first make one turn, timed by its first entry, exit or band change:
    - drums, bass or other coming in or stopping, marked when a stem stops for the rest of the song;
    - a playing stem's level stepping 6 dB or more, comparing the 4 s medians either side, at least 6 s apart and moved to the sharpest 50 ms change; a vocal step counts only when the vocal stem plays through both 4 s windows, since a phrase after a gap is a phrase, not a step;
    - the band (drums, bass and other) dropping out or coming back by 9 dB or more, comparing 2 s medians, with whether the vocal stem plays across it;
    - a loop break (step 12).
    Each turn reports the whole mix's change and the low end's (drums plus bass), as the median of the 8 s after minus the 8 s before, shortened near the ends. That's the sustained change a listener hears, not a 0.25 s jump.
11. **The drop.** Entries are turns where drums or bass come in or the band comes back, raising the low end 6 dB or more (on the Legendary Lovers edit the bass fades in under drums that already play, +1 dB, and that's not a drop). The drop is the first entry whose low-end rise is within 6 dB of the largest; `drops` lists every such entry, the drop first. Picking the largest alone would choose a later, slightly bigger return over the first time the song kicks in: in Crying During Sex, the final section's return at 204.59 is 1.4 dB bigger than the entry at 44.61.
12. **Loop breaks.** For drums, bass and other, each beat is turned into its log-mel spectrogram (48 bands) in four slices and correlated with the same beat 8 and 16 beats back, keeping the better (16 covers a beat grid tracked at double time). A stem that repeated itself (median correlation 0.8 or more over the two bars before) and stops matching (the next bar's median 0.12 or more lower) breaks its loop on the first beat whose own correlation falls. That needs the stem to play through the whole comparison, so entries and exits stay turns of their own. It is the only check that found the Excitin x 2On turn at 6.54, where every loop carries on and loudness barely moves, but a new layer comes into the other stem. On full songs it also marks where a part changes between sections.
13. **Stops.** On the mix at 50 ms: a dip of 0.25–4.5 s, at least 18 dB under the median of the 4 s before and 6 dB under the quietest 5% of the 8 s before, or 22 dB under the song's median out of a passage at normal level, followed by a return to within 6 dB of before. The context rule keeps a sparse song's routine gaps out. The fade rule still lets in near-silent phrase-end gaps on finger-picked guitar, so August 10 reports several of them.
14. **Audio sections.** Rough section boundaries come from a self-similarity matrix of beat-synchronous timbre (MFCC 1–12) and loudness. A checkerboard kernel is slid along its diagonal, with a half-width of a song's beats / 8, clamped to 4–16 beats. Peaks of at least 0.25 of the strongest, one kernel apart, are boundaries. Each moves to the nearest stem event within 1 s (a turn, a run edge or a stop's hit), which is named next to it. Chroma was left out: on the reference edit it tracked chord changes rather than the drop.
15. **Voice per section.** pyin on the vocal stem at 16 kHz with 20 ms frames, over frames inside the vocal stem's runs, above −50 dBFS and with a pitch. Each section gets the median pitch, p10–p90 and median level. A section with a p10–p90 range under 1.5 semitones or a median below A2 is marked unreliable: pyin has locked onto bass bleed (I Really Want to Stay at Your House's choruses read as a constant F#2). Between consecutive reliable sections, a move of 3 semitones or 4 dB is a voice shift. pyin still makes octave errors that this guard doesn't catch.
16. **Bass roots.** pyin on the bass stem at 8 kHz gives a pitch class per beat. A bar is four beats; the beat that starts a bar is the one of four that puts the most root changes on bar starts, since the beat tracker gives no downbeats. Each bar gets its most common pitch class, or none when under 30% of its frames carry a pitch. On White Light this gives A–C–E–D–A in two-bar steps, the chords under the hook.
17. **Key and tags.** Essentia's key extractor (EDMA profile) gives the song key and its strength. The Discogs-EffNet embedding gives the top two MTG-Jamendo instrument tags and the top Discogs-400 genre tag of the whole mix. The tags say what the mix sounds like, not whether a stem plays. On August 10 they report bass, which plays only some stretches, and on an isolated guitar they still report drums. The stems answer which parts play.
18. **Words.** faster-whisper runs on the vocal stem with `word_timestamps=True` and VAD off. Words closer than 1 s form a vocal phrase. On the Legendary Lovers edit, Whisper on the mix found 3 words; on the vocal stem it hears every line. The vocal stem can't tell film dialogue from singing.
19. **Report.** A video is split into sections at phrase edges and drops, each snapped to a shot boundary within 0.5 s. A song is split at the drop, its similar entries, and audio boundaries at least 1 s from them. Its table gives each section's beats, vocal phrase or vocal share, the stems playing through most of it, loudness, low band, the voice, and what it starts on. Every picture row is measured at its first frame; for a shot after a dissolve, that is the first blended frame. "On" means within 0.10 s. Events more than 1 s from a row are left out of the table but kept in `timeline.json`.

## Design choices

**Structure, not histograms, decides cuts.** Anime footage flickers inside shots: fireworks, lightning, explosion light, a face going from dark to lit. On the reference edit, flicker inside the fireworks-kiss shot scores a histogram distance of 0.66, as high as real cuts. Brightness-normalised structure stays above 0.86 correlation through that flicker and drops below 0.3 at cuts. The histogram distance and pixel difference are still recorded in `frames.json`. The pixel difference gates out noise on static dark frames. For a frame whose change stands out from everything near it, both serve as gates (step 3's second test): flicker never stands out that way, because it changes many frames in a row.

**Flashes and black are transitions, not shots.** They get their own rows so the table shows where each picture starts, and they never count towards shot totals or average shot length. A shot entered through a flash starts after the flash.

**Stems decide turns, not loudness jumps.** The old tool called any 0.25 s loudness jump of 6 dB or more a drop: 16 in Crying During Sex, and 16 in Exitin, a loop with no breaks at all, where they were gaps between bass notes. Stems name the change itself. The drums and bass entering at 44.61 is one turn with a +36 dB low-end rise, and the band falling away under the voice at 82.6–85.1 is another.

**Skipped from the trial.** allin1's sections and downbeats needed madmom from git and a stand-in for natten, and its verse/chorus labels were arbitrary on the slowcore songs. The mood models called Crying During Sex "relaxing / love". Essentia's per-beat chord labels on the full mix were noisy, and build detection found no real riser to test on. None of these is in the map.

**`small.en` with VAD off.** On the reference edit, multilingual `small` misses the quiet spoken intro entirely, and Silero VAD drops every vocal that sits under music. `small.en` without VAD recovers all three voices. Seven of the eight word times quoted in the breakdown come out within 0.1 s; the start of the second half of the last line comes out 0.3 s early. Pass `--whisper-model small` (and optionally `--lang`) for edits that aren't in English.

**ffmpeg pipes, not OpenCV.** opencv-python and PyAV both bundle libavdevice, which clash when loaded in one process (faster-whisper imports PyAV). Decoding through ffmpeg keeps OpenCV out of this script.

## Known gaps

Checked against the trial on the songs in `media/songs/`:
- **A new layer at the same level.** On the Legendary Lovers edit a layer comes in under the voice at 3.4 s. The other stem rises only 1–2 dB, but it gets about 5× noisier and 450 Hz brighter, and moves from G to E. Nothing in the map says so; only the novelty boundary at 3.55 marks it. A flatness and brightness step catches it, but at that setting it also fires 37–82 times per full song.
- **Short breaks.** A half-bar bass gap (Nothing Without You, 10.0–10.5) is too short for a stem run and too shallow for a stop.
- **Ducks under film dialogue.** The music dipping under a spoken line in an edit's audio isn't reported, and the vocal stem can't tell speech from singing.
- **Voice octave errors.** pyin's octave jumps and low bleed (for example A#2 in Exitin) can pass the voice guard and show up as voice shifts of 12 semitones or more.

## Against the hand-made breakdown

On `denreze-whitelight.mov` the script finds the breakdown's 19 shots. Every hard cut and flash it lists lands within one frame, as do the kiss's 2.85 s hold, the one cross-dissolve and 123 BPM. The drop lands on the 4.09 beat (drums up 14 dB, the band up 14 dB, bass in from 4.40), against the breakdown's 4.0. Where they differ, the script follows the frames:

- The opening fade-up is reported as 0.65 s of black before shot 1.
- The bloom at 3.95 stays in the city shot. The white flash proper runs 4.00–4.10.
- The hands-against-fireballs shot cuts to near-black at 11.75. A dark city shot then fades up and dissolves into the silhouette from 12.20 to 13.00. The breakdown has one shot running to about 12.2.
- The pin blast is a single bright frame at 14.10, after one black frame. The café shot flickers in from 14.15, not 14.30.
- One-frame black cuts at 7.85 and 18.45 get their own rows.

## Caching

Picture analysis, the audio map, the key and tags, and word timings are cached in `OUT_DIR/cache/` with the same signature scheme as `index.py`: source path, size and mtime, the relevant options, and a version per stage. A tagger that couldn't download its models isn't cached, so the next run retries. Stems are cached separately by content (step 8). Rerunning reuses whatever still matches. Sheets and reports are rebuilt every run.

## Tests

`tests/test_analyze_edit.py` runs on every invocation. It checks the segmentation on synthetic frames and, through ffmpeg, a cut between two shots alike in layout and palette that the distance test misses. On a synthetic 120 BPM track built from four synthetic stems it checks the audio map, stem runs, turns, the drop at 8 s, the voice shift, bass roots, the cache round trip, the audio offset and the audio-only CLI (with the stems placed in a temporary stem cache, so no model runs). It also checks that an mp3's cover art isn't read as video. `tests/test_music.py` covers each measurement on small synthetic stems: levels and edge refinement, entries, exits and band changes, faint stems, steps, the drop rule, stops and the context guard, a loop break, bass roots, the voice guard, the tempo flag, JSON round trips, and the model checksum. `tests/test_stems.py` covers the stem cache. With `denreze-whitelight.mov` at the repo root, or `ANALYZE_EDIT_VIDEO` pointing at it, it also checks the breakdown's ground truth within one frame: the hard cuts, the flashes, the kiss as one shot, the dissolve, the tempo and the drop. It also checks that the soundtrack, extracted to its own file, gives the same audio map.
