# Breakdown: Denji × Reze — "White Light"

- **Source:** *Chainsaw Man – The Movie: Reze Arc* (MAPPA, 2025)
- **Audio:** "White Light" by Sidewalks and Skeletons, with two lines of English film dialogue laid over it
- **File:** `denreze-whitelight.mov`. It runs 24.75 s at 1024×576 and 20 fps. The picture is 19.35 s and 19 shots; the rest is black with the editor's logo while the song rings out. About 123 BPM, with the drop at 4.0 s.
- Times are measured with `analyze_edit.py`. Lyrics and dialogue are paraphrased; the time-synced lyrics are on [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net/api/search?artist_name=Sidewalks%20and%20Skeletons&track_name=White%20Light).

Most of the detail is knowledge of the film (its plot, its characters, what fans feel about them), because that is what lets each moment carry its part of the edit's story for a fan. It's one reading of one edit, not a structure to follow.

## What's true about them

Reze is a Soviet assassin and a Bomb Devil hybrid, sent to get close to Denji and take his heart. She is the first girl who seems to want him rather than what he can do, and the wanting is the mission. She is, more or less, Denji's first love: his dream of a normal boy falling for a normal girl, and up to the festival he is living it.

At the festival she kisses him as the fireworks go off, and mid-kiss she bites off his tongue. That is how her attack starts. After cutting him down, she kisses him again and apologizes. She nearly kills him, and the morning after he still asks her to run away with him. She leaves. Then, at the station, she lets her train go and turns back for him at the café, which is why fans believe the feeling was real. Makima kills her in an alley before she gets there. He waits for her with a bouquet, and she never arrives.

The creator of the manga has said that love and explosions are alike: both are over in an instant and leave nothing behind.

## The song: how it feels and how it's built

"White Light" (2015) opens with a spoken skit. A woman talks a hesitant girl into trying a new drug called White Light, which supposedly makes you feel like you died and came back. When the girl wavers, the woman taunts her: is she scared of a little danger? Then the beat drops, and a chopped hook about burning out follows. Later the song goes inside the high: it's electric, every time feels like the first, there's a flash of panic, then reassurance.

In the user's words, it's about trying a new substance for the first time: it's a rush, it's scary, it's breathtaking.

## The edit's story

**What hits the user** is the expectation flipping. You're braced for carnage and get them falling for each other, like stepping on a mine in a minefield and finding out it was a carrot. It also lands because of what the kiss is in the film: the last moment of the ordinary romance. Up to the festival Denji has been living his dream of being a normal boy with a normal girl. Seconds later she bites his tongue and he's back to fighting for his life. For one moment, he was just a boy in love.

That is where the song and the film meet. In the user's words, the film's first love is dangerous but breathtaking, and the song is the rush of trying something for the first time. For these two the kiss *is* the danger.

One reading the edit supports: Reze is the drug, a high that feels like dying, offered as a dare, and Denji takes it. The film even asks him the dare outright. After the pool, Reze tells him the fable of the country mouse and the city mouse: a safe, dull life or a rich, dangerous one. Denji picks the city mouse because the food is better and it's more fun. The song's taunt is a question he already answered.

The edit also runs the film backwards. In the film the kiss turns into violence. Here violence turns into the kiss: she snaps her fingers to set off her bombs, the sparks rise over the city, and what goes off is the fireworks. That gives them back the one moment before the betrayal.

## The song's shape becomes the story's shape

The edit plays the song straight through from the end of the taunt: the drop, eight seconds of beat, then the hook coming in. Two film lines are laid over it. Each part of that stretch does one thing for the edit's story:

- **Under the dare (0–4.1):** the dare, asked over the danger itself.
- **The drop (4.1–6.95):** the dare is answered with the kiss, not the blast.
- **Denji's line (6.95–9.55):** he still likes her, while she tries to kill him.
- **The beat and the hook (9.55–16.3):** tender and violent on every cut, until the hook burns it out.
- **Reze's line (16.3–19.35):** her parting words, that he'll never learn. He'd take the dare again.

## The moments

Numbers match the shot table at the end.

### Under the dare (0–4.1)

- **1–2. Reze snaps her fingers upward, and her bombs' sparks rise over the night city.** It's one continuous stretch of the film, from near the end, not the start. The edit opens in the war, with Reze as the bomb. Nothing here is fireworks yet. The switch happens at the cut to the kiss, where sparks rising over a night city become fireworks bursting behind them. The director picked the most beautiful part of the orange range for the film's explosions, so the two match.

The taunt asks whether you're scared of danger while the picture shows the danger itself.

### The drop (4.1–6.95)

- **4. The fireworks kiss, held for 2.85 s.** Just before it, on the hill above the festival, Reze asks him to quit and run away with her and says she likes him. He's tempted but says no: he has a job and people he cares about now. The shot is the love story and the attack at once, and the last moment before everything turns. Fans know what happens a second later, so the edit never shows the bite.

### Denji's line (6.95–9.55)

The line is lifted out of the beach scene the morning after the fight and laid over two kinds of footage:

- **5. The night-school classroom.** Denji can't read, so Reze sneaks him into the empty school at night and plays teacher. It's their sweetest stretch. Only her last thoughts reveal that she had never been to school either. That's why she didn't kill him the night they met: she was as excited as he was.
- **6–7. The alley fight and the rooftop fight, both graded cold blue.** This is her trying to kill him. The turn in his line, "but I do", lands on the cut to the rooftop. He says he still likes her while she's blowing him up.

### The beat and the hook (9.55–16.3)

- **8. Reze in the school pool at night, from behind.** He admits he can't swim, and she teaches him. It's the film's showpiece scene, expanded from the manga.
  - The nudity in Chainsaw Man is usually Denji's crude dream. Here it plays as his coming of age, tender rather than leering, and her back is the most carefully drawn body in the scene.
  - The pool also has a second half every fan knows. In the final fight Denji reminds her she taught him to swim, chains himself to her and jumps into the sea, where she can't make the spark she needs to explode. The lesson she gave him is how he stops her.
  - The pool shares the blue grade of the fights, so the two read as the same night.
- **9–11. Her hands three times, on the beat.** First reaching up to the sky as the rain starts at the pool, then reaching forward in battle, then held against falling fireballs. The same hands, tender and then violent.
- **12. Her silhouette against a burning sky.** The hook about burning out comes in right here, over her standing in the fire.
- **14. She pulls the pin on her choker.** The choker is the one accessory she always wears. It hides the grenade pin she pulls to turn into the Bomb Devil.
- **15–16. The blast, then her smiling in the café, 0.2 s later.** The café, Crossroads, is where she works and where she invited him the night they met. At the end of the film it's where he waits for her with a bouquet, and she never arrives. The edit puts the detonator and the girl he fell for back to back. With the hook still going, the café smile is the thing burning out.
- **17. The flower handoff, with Makima's ringed eye composited behind it, for 5 frames.** This is the only image the editor made, and it takes the most knowledge to read:
  - Makima tells Denji he has a heart.
  - Moved by that, he gives money at a charity stand, gets a flower and eats it.
  - Rain drives him into a phone booth, Reze runs in laughing and then crying, and he coughs the flower up and gives it to her.
  - At the end she is leaving town when she sees the same stand. She gets a flower of her own, remembers his, lets her train go and walks toward the café.
  - Makima is waiting in the alley on the way there and kills her before she arrives.

  The flower comes from Makima at the start, and Makima ends it. Her eye isn't in that scene in the film. Putting it behind the handoff says she was watching all along, and at 5 frames it plays like a thought the viewer can't push away.
- **18. Aki, glancing sideways.** He's the one who yelled at Denji not to fall for her when she claimed her feelings were real.

### Reze's line (16.3–19.35)

- **19. Chainsaw Man vs. Bomb.** The final fight, as her line begins.
- **20. Two small figures by the sea, on the word that calls him a boy.** The beach the morning after. She told him she never liked him and it was all training; he said he liked her anyway, because she taught him to swim. Then she hugged him, broke his neck and left. Both lines in the edit come from this scene: his is what he says there, and hers is her parting words as she leaves him. So the edit's split conversation ends where it actually happened.
- **21. An extreme close-up of her eye, for the second half of her line.** The line lands on the person saying it.

She says he'll never learn: he'd take the dare again. A fan also knows what she doesn't say. She turned back for the café herself.

### What it leaves to the fan

The tongue bite, the drowning, the alley and the empty café are all missing, and Makima appears only as an eye. The edit isn't in the film's order either. It works in 19 seconds because it's made for people who've seen the film: each shot only has to point at a scene, and the viewer brings the rest. That's how five frames can carry the whole ending.

## The craft

### How it's cut

The cutting follows the song. It's slow under the dare, holds through the drop, then speeds up as the hook comes in, with four cuts in about a second at the loudest bars, and slows again for Reze's line. Montage cuts land on beats. Cuts under the dialogue land on words, so each line turns where the picture changes. White flashes mark the big hits. The picture ends before the song does.

- The taunt's last word ends at 3.92, a white flash hits the drop, and the kiss comes in.
- The kiss is the longest shot in the edit, held for 2.85 s through the loudest stretch while everything around it cuts faster. So the shot that is the love story and the attack at once gets the most time.
- After Denji's line ends, the picture dips to black before the pool.
- The cross-dissolve into her silhouette against the burning sky (12) is the edit's only soft transition.
- The flower handoff's composite (17) is hand-animated across two frames: the joined hands and the flower shift slightly, so it doesn't read as a still. You'd only notice the motion going frame by frame.
- The picture cuts to black right after Reze's last word, and the caption stays a moment longer.

### What the editor did to the song

Measured by lining the edit's audio (`media/songs/White Light - Sidewalks and Skeletons (edit).mp3`) up against the original track (`… (full).mp3`) and comparing loudness in low (below 150 Hz), mid and high (above 4 kHz) bands every 0.25 s. Edit time *t* is original time *t* + 29.25 s.

- **No cuts or speed change.** The edit plays the original from 29.25 s to 54.0 s continuously at 1×. The drop at 4.0 s is the song's own drop (33.25 s in the original).
- **A low tone under the taunt.** In the original the taunt has almost nothing below 150 Hz. The edit has a steady tone around 50 Hz from about 1.2 s, about when Reze snaps her fingers, to the drop. It eases off slightly in the last half second. It's a held tone, not broadband noise. Whether the editor added it or it comes from the film's sound under the snap is unknown.
- **Louder and denser after the drop.** From 4.0 s the edit averages about 2 dB louder, with about 2 dB less dynamic range (peak to average) and more clipped samples. The bass between kick hits is 5–10 dB louder than in the original, while the kicks themselves are about the same. The hit at 4.0 s is only about 2 dB louder than the original's.
- **Highs cut under the film lines.** During Denji's line (about 7.0–9.2 s) and Reze's (about 16.3–19.0 s), the music's highs are 10–20 dB below the original while its bass is the same or louder. It's a filter, not a volume duck. The highs come back at 9.25 s, right after Denji's line.
- **A muffled ring-out.** From 19.3 s, as the picture ends, the highs fall away completely over about 2 s (to 60–70 dB below the original), and the overall level drops 4–8 dB.
- **Highs otherwise slightly lower.** Outside those stretches the edit's highs sit about 2 dB below the original's.

The renderer can make changes like these: song keyframes for volume, bass, compression and low-pass, and `muffle_hz` on a dialogue layer. See [Shaping the song](../docs/pipeline.md#shaping-the-song).
